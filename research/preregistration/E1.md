# E1 preregistration: plan batching, prediction gating and the adaptive arm

Status: registered before any evaluation run.

## Question

An agent learning hidden rules from observation can be allowed to:
- act one step per call (`single`);
- commit to a batch of actions (`plan`);
- batch actions with a prediction of each one's effect, stopping at the first wrong
  prediction (`predict`);
- switch from predicting to batching once its predictions keep coming true (`adaptive`).

Under equal credit ceilings, which arm clears more of an unseen game, and does the answer
depend on the budget?

## Design

- **Games:** `relay`, `glide`, `twins` (`flowproof/testbed`).
  - All three are original. Each has 4 levels, and its hidden rule changes from level
    to level.
  - The model sees only observations, never game code, and every transcript is checked
    for each game's canary.
- **Seeds:** 0, 1, 2. They are evaluation-only, distinct from development seeds 100
  and 101.
- **Arms:** `single`, `plan`, `predict`, `adaptive`, with `K = 12` steps per call,
  `M = 4` verified predictions before switching, and a history window of 6.
- **Budgets:** 1.5, 4 and 10 credits per episode.
  - A ledger enforces the ceiling, plus a cap of 150 calls.
  - The ledger is checked between calls, and each call is capped by the provider at
    `max(30, remaining)` credits.
- **Model:** `claude-haiku-5.5` at default reasoning effort, through the Copilot CLI
  harness with no tools and no workspace. The call timeout is 300 s.
- **Size:** 3 games × 3 seeds × 4 arms × 3 budgets, which is 108 episodes. The worst case
  is 558 credits (the sum of the ceilings).

## Outcomes

- **Primary:** episode score.
  - For each cleared level, efficiency is `min(1, optimal / actions)`, where `optimal`
    is the breadth-first-search optimum.
  - The episode score is the mean weighted by level number, and uncleared levels
    score 0.
- **Secondary:**
  - levels cleared;
  - calls and credits per episode;
  - credits per cleared level;
  - prediction accuracy (`predict` and `adaptive`);
  - invalid calls;
  - stop reasons.

## Analysis

- **Unit:** the (game, seed) pair. There are 9 units per budget, and arms are compared
  within each unit.
- **Contrasts at each budget:** `adaptive − plan`, `adaptive − predict` and
  `predict − plan`.
  - Each is reported as a mean paired difference with a bootstrap 95% interval
    (10,000 resamples, seed 0) and an exact sign-flip permutation p-value (512 sign
    patterns).
  - The 9 tests (3 contrasts × 3 budgets) are adjusted with Holm's method.
- **Pre-stated directions:**
  - H1: `adaptive ≥ plan` and `adaptive ≥ predict`.
  - H2: `predict − plan` differs between the lowest and highest budget.
  - H3: `single` is lowest at every budget.
- **Episodes ending `accounting-unknown`:** these are episodes where a call's spend could
  not be measured, for example a timeout. They stay in the primary analysis with the
  score they had reached, and a sensitivity analysis excludes them. Episodes are never
  re-run selectively.

## Disclosed before registration

These results were seen on development seed 100 (one episode per game and arm, 3 credits)
before the design was fixed.
- **Mean score:** adaptive 0.35, predict 0.18, plan 0.14, single 0.03.
- **Cost:** about 0.15–0.19 credits per call.
- **Choice of model:** a cost probe on one identical prompt measured `claude-haiku-5.5` at
  0.06 credits per call. Other models cost 0.2–2.7. That probe chose the model and the
  budget levels.
- **Discarded results:** an earlier development preflight lost most of its results to a
  record-naming bug, now fixed. Its surviving episodes ran other models and are not used.

## Limits

- **Scope:** one model, three small games and three seeds per game.
- **The adaptive arm's settings:** `M` was not tuned.
- **What scores mean:** they measure efficiency against an optimal solver, not against
  human players.
